## Before we start: Understanding Users vs. Members

**Permit.io** has two kinds of identities: **Users** and **Members**.

:::tip
**Users** are the people using your app. **Members** are your team, managing Permit.
:::

### Users

Users are the identities (human or automated) whose access to your application you control. A user
can belong to multiple tenants and hold roles in each.

Example:

> Sarah uses your app and belongs to the "Marketing" tenant. To create content, she needs the "Editor" role in that tenant.

### Members

Members are your team members working within the Permit app, governed by Permit's own access control (Authorization for Authorization).
By default, only members can access Permit and author or edit policies.

## Before we start: When to sync your users?

Sync users into Permit during sign-in or sign-up, right after your authentication
provider verifies the user's identity. At that point, map their organizational role (for example Head of Marketing,
Customer, Consultant, or Patient) and the relevant claims in their JSON Web Token (JWT) to application-level roles,
attributes, and relationships.

Syncing at this point means a user's permissions match their role from their first request.

## Syncing Users into Permit

There are three ways to add users to Permit:

1. **User Management Screen**: add and manage users in the Permit dashboard.

2. **`syncUser` Function in SDKs**: call `syncUser` from your code during application
   workflows such as sign-up or profile updates.

3. **API**: call the Permit API from your own workflows or backend processes.

## Bulk User Synchronization

To move an existing user database into Permit, use one of two approaches.

### Script-Based Bulk Syncing

Write a script that uses the Permit API or SDK to iterate over your users and push them all into Permit. Every user
is available in Permit right away, but you write and run the script, and you handle errors and data inconsistencies
yourself.

### Incremental Syncing at Login (Recommended)

Sync each user at the handoff point instead: during login, as soon as the user's identity is verified.

You don't preload the whole dataset, and only active users are synced.
As users log in over time, your active user base ends up in Permit.

:::tip KEY ADVANTAGE
Incremental syncing keeps Permit current and skips inactive or outdated users.
:::

The handoff point is the middleware step between authentication and authorization:

---

#### Authentication Confirmation

> The authentication provider verifies the user's identity.

#### Session and JWT Issuance

> A session is created, and a JWT containing the user's unique identifier is issued.

#### User Synchronization

> The user's identity, derived from the JWT, is synced into Permit.io as a new user entity.

#### Role Assignment

> The system assigns an appropriate role to the user based on the application's policies.

## SCIM for automation

SCIM (System for Cross-domain Identity Management) automates user management at scale.

### Why Use SCIM?

- **Efficiency**: Automates user provisioning and updates.
- **Consistency**: Ensures uniform user data across systems.
- **Security**: Minimizes manual interventions.

### How SCIM Works

SCIM (System for Cross-domain Identity Management) connects identity providers (IdPs) like Okta to service providers
(SPs) such as Permit.io, and transfers user identity data between them automatically. When something changes
in the IdP, such as a new hire, a role change, or an account deactivation, the change is pushed to the SP.

> For example, Lisa joins your company. HR creates her profile in your IdP, such as
> Okta, with her name, email address, and role, "Marketing Specialist". SCIM sends Lisa's
> information to Permit.io, which creates her user and assigns her predefined roles.

> When Lisa first logs in to the app, she already has the access she needs, without manual work from IT or administrators.
> If her role changes or her account is deactivated, SCIM sends those updates too.

With SCIM, your IdP stays the source of truth for users, and Permit follows it.

## What did you learn?

You now understand:

- **The Difference Between Users and Members**: Users are the end-users of your application, while Members are your team managing
  Permit.io itself.

- **The Importance of Syncing Users at the Right Time**: Syncing during sign-in or sign-up gives users the right roles
  and permissions from their first request.

- **Methods to Sync Users**: Use the User Management Screen to add users manually, `syncUser` in the SDKs from your code, or the Permit
  API from your own workflows.

- **Bulk User Synchronization**: Incremental syncing at login is the recommended method, because it
  syncs only active users.

- **SCIM Integration**: SCIM automates user management at scale and keeps Permit in step with your IdP.
